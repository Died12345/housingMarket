## Scope and epistemic caveat

None of this evidence is dispositive either way — it's mostly indirect (psychology of belief, effects of ritual/prayer on health outcomes) rather than direct evidence bearing on God's existence. Flagging this once rather than per-section. Inferences from me (not the sources) are marked **[inference]**.

---

## EVIDENCE CLAIMED FOR

### Intercessory prayer (IP) studies

**Byrd 1988** (Southern Medical Journal) — 393 CCU patients, randomized (192 prayer / 201 control), double-blind. Prayer group had lower "severity score" (composite of complications) p<.01; multivariate separation p<.0001; control patients needed ventilatory support, antibiotics, diuretics more often.
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3393937/) · [full text PDF](https://www.gpposner.com/Byrd_study.pdf)

- **Critique (Posner):** randomization was not blinded from the *statistician* scoring severity in real time; the severity-index weighting was constructed post hoc by Byrd himself, not a validated instrument; contamination uncontrolled — patients in both arms could be prayed for by family/church outside the protocol, so the "control" was never a no-prayer group, only a no-*additional*-prayer group. [gpposner.com](https://www.gpposner.com/Byrd_study.html)

**Harris et al. 1999** (Archives of Internal Medicine) — ~1,000 CCU patients, randomized by odd/even chart number (not computer-randomized — weaker allocation concealment than Byrd), double-blind. Prayer group had lower "CCU course score" (composite); no difference in length of stay or mortality. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10547166/) · [JAMA Network](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/485161)

**STEP (Benson et al. 2006, American Heart Journal)** — largest, most rigorous IP trial: 1,802 CABG patients, 6 hospitals, 3 arms: (1) prayed-for/uncertain (n=604), (2) not-prayed-for/uncertain (n=597), (3) prayed-for/certain (n=601). Complications: 52% (uncertain+prayer) vs. 51% (uncertain+no-prayer) — no difference. Certain-of-prayer group: 59% complications vs. 52% in the uncertain-prayer group — **worse**, and this was the one statistically significant result in the study. 30-day mortality similar across all three arms. Authors' own interpretation: certainty of being prayed for may have induced performance anxiety ("performance pressure") rather than any benefit from prayer itself. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16569567/) · [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002870305006496) · [Harvard Gazette summary](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2006/04/prayers-dont-help-heart-surgery-patients-2/)

**Meta-analyses:**
- Masters, Spielmans & Goodson 2006 (Annals of Behavioral Medicine) — 14 RCTs of distant IP, pooled effect size **g = 0.10, not significantly different from zero**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16827626/)
- Cochrane review (Roberts et al., latest update 2009, CD000368) — 10 trials, 7,646 participants. Conclusion: "equivocal," "does not support a recommendation either in favour or against." [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD000368.pub3/abstract)
  - **Critique (Jørgensen, Hróbjartsson & Gøtzsche 2009, J Negative Results Biomed):** two of the ten trials driving the pooled result had undisclosed problems — most notably the Cha/Lobo "retroactive prayer" trial where prayer was administered *4–10 years after* patients had already left the hospital (dead or alive), which the reviewers argue should never have been pooled with prospective trials at all. They call Cochrane's neutral "more research needed" framing scientifically incoherent given that a positive finding would require backward causation. [J Negative Results Biomed](https://jnrbm.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1477-5751-8-7)

**Bottom line:** the largest and best-controlled trial (STEP) found no benefit and a possible negative certainty effect; meta-analyses centre on a null effect size; positive early trials (Byrd, Harris) have documented methodological weaknesses (unblinded severity scoring, uncontrolled contamination, weak randomization). Consensus among most reviewers outside religious-studies/CAM journals: **no credible evidence of an IP effect on physical health outcomes.**

### Near-death experiences (NDEs)

**AWARE (Parnia et al. 2014, Resuscitation)** — 2,060 cardiac arrests across 15 hospitals, UK/US. Of resuscitated survivors able to complete structured interviews, **39% reported some perception/awareness without explicit recall; only 9% reported experiences meeting NDE criteria; 2% reported full awareness with explicit recall compatible with OBE claims.** One patient's account of resuscitation-room sounds was assessed as broadly compatible with real events (auditory, not the popularized "shoe on the ledge" visual-verification story, which comes from a separate, non-AWARE case reported by Kimberly Clark Sharp). No patient in AWARE was shown to have accurately reported the specific visual "hidden target" images placed in the study rooms — too few of the interviewed survivors had experienced cardiac arrest in a room fitted with a target shelf. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25301715/) · [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/10/141007092108.htm)

**AWARE-II (Parnia et al. 2023, Resuscitation)** — 567 in-hospital cardiac arrests (US/UK), **only 53 (9.3%) survived, 28 (52.8% of survivors) completed interviews, 11 (39.3% of those interviewed) reported memories/perceptions suggestive of consciousness.** Of these, ~20% reported features consistent with a "recalled experience of death" (RED); ~3% had audiovisual awareness. Of visual/auditory tasks presented via headphones/tablet during CPR, **1/28 (3.6%) correctly identified the auditory stimulus** — a single hit, not independently distinguishable from chance/guessing given the tiny denominator. Authors' interpretive framing (disinhibition/"new dimensions of reality" giving access to lucid memory) is speculative, not an experimental finding. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37423492/) · [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/09/230914175140.htm)

- **[inference]** Both AWARE trials are underpowered for the veridical-perception question specifically — total confirmed "hit" count across both studies is effectively n=1–2 out of tens of thousands of cardiac arrests screened. This is consistent with a base rate near what guessing/partial-consciousness-during-CPR would predict; it is not evidence for consciousness separate from a functioning brain.

**Pam Reynolds case (1991, not a controlled study — single case report popularized by Sabom)** — hypothermic cardiac arrest ("standstill") surgery; patient later reported OOBE details (bone saw, conversation). Skeptical explanation (anesthesiologist Gerald Woerlee): standstill was not instantaneous — there were periods of residual brain activity/anesthesia awareness before and after full standstill during which auditory information (imperfectly masked by earplugs/clicking) could have been picked up; the "bone saw" description matches a dental-drill-like sound rather than requiring visual perception. Not independently verified under controlled conditions; anecdote, not data. [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pam_Reynolds_case) · [CRI apologetics response for contrast](https://www.equip.org/articles/did-pam-reynolds-have-a-near-death-experience/)

**Neuroscientific explanations for NDE phenomenology:**
- **Dying-brain gamma surge:** Borjigin et al. 2013 (PNAS) — continuous EEG in rats during induced cardiac arrest found a **transient surge of highly synchronized, coherent gamma oscillations in the first ~30 seconds post-arrest**, preceding isoelectric (flat) EEG, with increased anterior-posterior connectivity and cross-frequency coupling to theta/alpha — patterns associated with conscious/feature-binding states while awake. Interpreted as a plausible neural correlate for vivid, "hyper-real" subjective experience during the dying process, i.e., the brain *can* generate intense conscious-like activity as it's shutting down, without needing anything supernatural. [PNAS 2013](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1308285110). Follow-up in humans: Borjigin et al. 2023 (PNAS) found similar gamma surges in dying human ICU patients whose life support was withdrawn. [PNAS 2023](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2216268120)
- **DMT hypothesis (Strassman):** speculative — endogenous pineal DMT release under extreme stress proposed as mechanism for NDE-like phenomenology. **Critique:** no evidence of sufficient endogenous DMT concentration in dying humans to produce psychoactive effects; Potts & Does (2012) found the phenomenological match between DMT trip reports and the standard NDE scale weaker than popularly claimed. [Frontiers overview](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1532937/full) · [PMC review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6107838/)
- **REM intrusion:** Nelson et al. 2006 (Neurology) — of 55 people reporting an NDE, **60% also had a history of REM intrusion** (dream content bleeding into waking consciousness, e.g. sleep paralysis/hypnagogic hallucination) vs. **24% of 55 matched controls**. Proposed shared mechanism: an arousal-system predisposition (vagal tone) drives both REM intrusion and NDE susceptibility. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16606911/) · [Neurology](https://n.neurology.org/content/66/7/1003.short)
- **[inference]** These three mechanisms (gamma surge, hypoxia/anoxia effects generally, REM intrusion) are not mutually exclusive and jointly cover most reported NDE phenomenology (tunnel/light, life review, OOBE sensation, bliss) via known neurophysiology, without requiring anything non-physical. None of them rules out that NDEs *feel* subjectively real and meaningful — that's a separate (psychological) question from whether they reflect an external, mind-independent afterlife.

### Investigated miracle claims

**Lourdes:** ~7,000 healings self-reported since 1858; only **72 recognized as miraculous by the Catholic Church** as of the most recent case (2025) — roughly **1 in 97 reported cures**, or against total pilgrim traffic (~80,000/year sick/disabled visitors historically), an official cure rate near **1 in 3 million visits**. Full medical investigation takes 5–12 years, standard is: instantaneous, complete, permanent, without medical explanation. [Wikipedia: Lourdes Medical Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lourdes_Medical_Bureau) · [Psi Encyclopedia](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/lourdes-cures/)
- **Base-rate problem [inference from search results, not a single primary source]:** spontaneous remission occurs at non-trivial rates for many conditions (MS has documented spontaneous remission episodes in a majority of relapsing-remitting cases at some point; even rare spontaneous regression of some cancers is estimated at roughly 1 in 60,000–100,000). With ~80,000 sick visitors/year over 165+ years (~13 million visitor-years), a "1-in-3-million" cure rate is not obviously above what spontaneous remission plus diagnostic uncertainty would predict on its own — the Medical Bureau's screening filters out most spontaneous-remission-compatible cases but a 2013 retrospective analysis found only the **25 post-1947 cases** resistant to reclassification as known spontaneous-remission types under modern diagnostic standards. This is a much smaller "hard core" of unexplained cases than the "72 miracles" headline figure suggests — most of the 72 predate rigorous modern diagnostics.

**Vatican canonization process:** requires 2 confirmed miracles (beatification + canonization), typically medical cures, reviewed by the **Consulta Medica** (~100 physicians, panel of 5 reviews each case; under Pope Francis's 2016 reform, 2/3 of the panel must affirm no natural explanation exists) before theological consultors assess whether it was obtained through the intercession of the specific candidate (not provable, taken on faith). [USC/The Conversation overview](https://theconversation.com/whats-a-miracle-heres-how-the-catholic-church-decides-170183) · [EWTN](https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/why-miracles-are-required-for-canonizations-5992)
- **[inference]** The medical panel's "no natural explanation" finding is a negative/exclusionary claim (medicine can't currently explain it), not a positive test for supernatural causation, and is vulnerable to the same base-rate/selection issue as Lourdes: millions of prayer requests are made to Catholic saints; only cases with an unusually good outcome get submitted for review at all (survivorship bias built into the process).

**Craig Keener's *Miracles: The Credibility of the New Testament Accounts* (2011, 2 vols., ~1,172 pp.)** — compiles hundreds of modern eyewitness-reported miracle claims worldwide (mostly healings) to argue NT miracle accounts are historically plausible as eyewitness reports (not necessarily veridical supernatural events, just that ancient reports of purported miracles reflect genuine eyewitness testimony rather than late legendary invention). [Themelios review](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/review/miracles-the-credibility-of-the-new-testament-accounts/)
- **Evidential-quality critique:** Keener's stated goal is historiographical (were these reports sincere eyewitness testimony?), not diagnostic (were the underlying events actually supernatural?) — reviewers note the book does almost no independent medical verification of the claims it compiles. Slade's methodological critique: Keener doesn't apply standard testimony-evaluation tools from forensics/law (e.g., Criteria-Based Content Analysis, the ADVOKATE framework) to filter for reliability, and shows "overt enthusiasm" that undercuts independent verification. Loftus's counter (*The Case Against Miracles*, 2019): testimony to an *a priori* improbable event needs correspondingly strong corroboration; anecdote volume ("hundreds of claims") doesn't substitute for verification of any single case, and rare-but-mundane events (spontaneous remission, misdiagnosis, regression to the mean) are common enough that "unexplained by the treating physician" is a low bar. [Infidels review of Loftus/Keener debate](https://infidels.org/library/modern/gregory-michna-loftus-miracles/)

### Psychedelic/mystical experience research (Griffiths et al., Johns Hopkins)

**Griffiths et al. 2006 (Psychopharmacology)** — 36 hallucinogen-naïve adults, double-blind, psilocybin (high dose) vs. active placebo (methylphenidate), within an explicitly "supportive setting." **Result:** psilocybin session ranked by most participants among the top 5 most personally meaningful/spiritually significant experiences of their lives; effects assessed by the Pahnke-Richards Mystical Experience Questionnaire, scores comparable to descriptions of classic mystical/religious experiences reported historically. At **14-month follow-up: 58%/67% still rated it among top-5 meaningful/spiritually significant; 64% reported increased well-being.** [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16826400/) · [2008 14-month follow-up](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0269881108094300)

**Griffiths et al. 2011 (dose-response) and 2018 (with meditation/spiritual practice)** — dose-related increases in mystical-type experience scores; 2018 paper found psilocybin + structured spiritual practice produced durable (months-scale) increases in prosocial trait measures. [SAGE 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0269881117731279)

- **What it shows [inference, standard interpretation in the literature]:** a specific serotonergic (5-HT2A agonist) pharmacological intervention reliably and reproducibly induces experiences phenomenologically indistinguishable from spontaneously-reported religious/mystical experiences (unity, sacredness, ineffability, noetic quality) in a majority of naive subjects under the right set/setting — strong evidence that the *brain's own machinery* is sufficient to generate God/unity-type experiences without any external stimulus, and that "feeling you encountered the divine" is not on its own good evidence that you did (it's inducible by a chemical). This doesn't logically disprove that some mystical experiences (drug-free ones) are veridical encounters with something real — it undercuts using the *phenomenology itself* (vividness, sense of certainty, ineffability) as evidence of veridicality, since a drug can produce the identical phenomenology with a known non-supernatural cause.

## EVIDENCE CLAIMED AGAINST

### Problem of evil

**Logical version (Mackie 1955) vs. Plantinga's free will defense (1974/1977):** Mackie argued God's omnipotence + omniscience + omnibenevolence is *logically* incompatible with evil's existence. Plantinga's free will defense shows this incompatibility isn't strict — it's logically possible that a world with free creatures and (some) evil is better than any world with free creatures and no evil, or that "transworld depravity" makes it impossible for God to actualize a world with free creatures who always freely choose good. **Mackie himself conceded in *The Miracle of Theism* (1982)** that Plantinga's defense is formally successful: "we can concede that the problem of evil does not, after all, show that the central doctrines of theism are logically inconsistent with one another" — while still doubting it's a *satisfying* solution. [IEP: Logical Problem of Evil](https://iep.utm.edu/evil-log/) · [Wikipedia: Plantinga's free-will defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Plantinga's_free-will_defense)
- Current consensus among philosophers of religion (both theist and atheist), per SEP/IEP surveys: the strict logical argument is **widely regarded as unsuccessful** post-Plantinga; serious debate has moved to the evidential version.

**Evidential version:**
- **Rowe 1979** — the "Bambi" case: a fawn horribly burned in a lightning-caused forest fire, suffering for days before dying, apparently to no purpose anyone can identify. Argument form: (1) probably no good we know of justifies God permitting this suffering; (2) an omniscient/omnipotent/perfectly good being would prevent any suffering unless it was necessary for some greater good; (3) therefore it's likely no such being exists. This is inductive/probabilistic, not a strict contradiction. [IEP: Evidential Problem of Evil](https://iep.utm.edu/evil-evi/)
- **Draper 1989** ("Pain and Pleasure: An Evidential Problem for Theists") — Bayesian framing. Defines the "Hypothesis of Indifference" (HI): if non-human persons/forces exist, they're indifferent to creature welfare (roughly, naturalism). Draper argues the actual observed *distribution* of pain/pleasure (much pain that isn't biologically useful; pleasure/pain patterns matching evolutionary function far better than a loving creator's) is better predicted by HI than by theism, i.e., **P(observations | HI) > P(observations | theism)**, which by Bayes counts as evidence favoring HI over theism even without a knockdown disproof. [PhilPapers entry](https://philpapers.org/rec/DRAPAP)

**Skeptical theism** (main response) — humans' cognitive limits ("noseeum" reasoning) mean our inability to identify a God-justifying reason for a given evil is weak evidence there is none; formalized via principles like CORNEA (Wykstra). [SEP: Skeptical Theism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/skeptical-theism/)
- **Costs/objections:** (1) if applied consistently, skeptical theism seems to undercut *any* inference from apparent gratuitous evil to "probably no God," but by parity also undercuts moral reasoning generally (if we can't trust our sense that a given horror is *pointless*, can we trust our sense that anything is good/bad enough to act on?) — the "moral paralysis" objection. (2) CORNEA-style epistemic principles risk global skepticism/regress. (3) It concedes the evidential force of specific evils while blocking only the inference, which many find an ad hoc, un-falsifiable move — theism becomes compatible with literally any amount/distribution of suffering. [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/skeptical-theism/)

### Divine hiddenness (Schellenberg)

Argument: if a perfectly loving God exists, God would ensure no capable, non-resistant person ever lacks belief that God exists (since belief is a precondition for a personal relationship). Some people are demonstrably non-resistant nonbelievers (they've genuinely sought and remain unconvinced) → therefore no perfectly loving God exists. [SEP: Hiddenness of God](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-hiddenness/) · [IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/divine-hiddenness-argument-against-gods-existence/)

**Responses:**
- **Murray** (soul-making/free-will preservation): too much revelation would coerce belief and remove morally significant freedom to accept/reject God.
- **Moser**: hiddenness ensures relationship isn't "cheap"; God wants filial trust/obedience, not mere propositional belief, and may withhold self-revelation from those disposed toward "cognitive idolatry."
- **[inference, standard counter in the literature]:** Both responses struggle with cases of genuinely earnest, non-resistant seekers (e.g., people raised devout who deconvert after years of prayer/study) — Schellenberg's argument is specifically built around this class of case, which free-will/soul-making responses don't obviously address, since belief-formation isn't fully voluntary and inquiry that is not itself resistant still often fails to produce belief.

### Cognitive science of religion (CSR)

- **HADD (Hyperactive/Hypersensitive Agency Detection Device)** — Barrett's proposal that humans evolved a bias toward over-attributing agency to ambiguous stimuli (rustling bush = predator, not wind) because false positives are cheap and false negatives are fatal; extended to explain why humans readily infer invisible agents (spirits, gods) behind unexplained events. [Secular Frontier summary](https://secularfrontier.infidels.org/2011/10/justin-barretts-hyperactive-agency-detection-device-hadd/)
- **Boyer** — religious concepts are cognitively "sticky" because they're *minimally counterintuitive* (mostly match intuitive ontology, but violate one salient expectation — e.g., an agent who is invisible or knows everything), which makes them memorable and transmissible independent of truth.
- **Debunking argument:** if belief in gods is a predictable by-product of belief-forming faculties (HADD, agency detection, minimal-counterintuitiveness bias) that were selected for unrelated survival reasons and are known to be unreliable in many other domains (pareidolia, apophenia), this undercuts the reliability of the process that produces religious belief.
- **Genetic fallacy caveat (important, and often glossed over in pop-atheist framing):** explaining the *causal origin* of a belief does not by itself show the belief is *false* — that's the genetic fallacy. Launonen 2021 (Zygon) and others argue CSR-based debunking arguments need an additional premise (that the belief-forming mechanism is unreliable *specifically with respect to the truth in question*, not just that it exists for other reasons) to avoid this fallacy, and that CSR alone doesn't establish that additional premise — it shows *how* belief is easy to form, not that it's *false*. [Zygon 2021, Launonen](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/zygo.12683) · [background piece](https://www.zygonjournal.org/article/14362/galley/29101/download/)
- **[inference]** Net assessment: CSR is decent evidence that humans have a strong *disposition* to form god-beliefs regardless of whether gods exist (a "no-cost" cognitive default), which somewhat weakens the evidential value of "religious belief is widespread" as an argument *for* God (see next section) — but on its own, CSR is not a complete debunking argument; it needs to be paired with an independent case that the relevant cognitive process (HADD, teleological bias) is truth-insensitive in this domain, which is separately supportable (HADD is demonstrably truth-insensitive for mundane agency attribution — that's its whole mechanism) but is an additional argumentative step, not a freebie from CSR data alone.

### Geographic/cultural distribution of religious belief

Pew's Global Religious Landscape data: religious affiliation correlates overwhelmingly with birthplace/region — Christians concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa (31%), Latin America-Caribbean (24%), Europe (22%); Muslims concentrated in Asia-Pacific/MENA; the religiously unaffiliated concentrated in East Asia, Europe, North America. [Pew 2012](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2012/12/18/global-religious-landscape-exec/) · [Pew 2017 update](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2017/04/05/the-changing-global-religious-landscape/)

**Argument from religious diversity/geography (informal, not attributable to a single named philosopher in the sources found — [inference: closest formal analogue is part of Draper's and Schellenberg's broader arguments, but the "accident of birth" framing is more a popular/informal argument than a peer-reviewed one]):** if a specific religion were true and its God wanted to be known, belief in that specific religion should not track birthplace/culture as strongly as it demonstrably does — a Thai-born person and a Texas-born person raised in their respective ambient religions overwhelmingly retain them, which is better explained by cultural transmission than by differential access to truth. [Religions Wiki summary of the argument](https://religions.wiki/index.php/Geographic_argument_against_religion)
- **[inference]** This is evidence against the reliability of religious belief-formation as a truth-tracking process (parallel to CSR's point), and specifically against the probability of any one *particular* religion's exclusive truth claims, but it's much weaker evidence against bare theism (a generic God existing) since bare theism doesn't make the same falsifiable predictions about uniform global convergence.

### Failure of prophecy / unanswered prayer base rates

- **Jehovah's Witnesses** — Watch Tower Society issued specific end-times date predictions (1799, 1844, 1874, 1878, 1918, 1925, 1975), each falsified; organization's current position is that it never claimed to be an "inspired prophet." [jwfacts.com list](https://www.jwfacts.com/watchtower/1800s.php) · [overview](https://jamesbishopblog.com/2018/12/03/the-jehovah-witness-the-problem-of-failed-prophecy/)
- **[inference]** repeated, specific, falsifiable religious prophecy failures (not unique to JWs — Millerites/Great Disappointment 1844 is the classic parallel case) are best explained as ordinary human error in a belief system insulated from falsification by post hoc reinterpretation, which is a standard case study in the psychology of cognitive dissonance (Festinger's *When Prophecy Fails*, 1956) rather than direct evidence against generic theism — it bears on the reliability of specific prophetic/institutional claims, not on God's existence per se.
- **Prayer efficacy base rates:** as covered above, rigorously controlled IP trials show null-to-negative effects; large observational studies linking "religiousness"/prayer frequency to health/mortality (e.g., the PMC study on private prayer frequency and 6-year survival) are confounded by the healthy-adherent effect, socioeconomic status, and social support — they measure correlates of a religious lifestyle, not causal efficacy of prayer as intercession. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11319365/) · [CRI's own methodological critique of this literature](https://www.equip.org/articles/the-problem-with-prayer-research/) (notable: even an apologetics-friendly source concedes the research base is weak).

## Summary assessment

- **For:** every empirical category claimed as evidence for God (IP, NDEs, miracles, mystical experience) has a well-evidenced naturalistic alternative explanation that fits the data at least as well, and in the best-controlled cases (STEP, AWARE, Griffiths) the naturalistic account fits *better*. None of these literatures, read on their own terms by the researchers who ran them, claims to have found proof of God — Parnia, Griffiths, and the Cochrane authors all stop well short of that in their own papers.
- **Against:** the logical problem of evil is dead (conceded by its own originator); the live debate is the evidential problem of evil and divine hiddenness, both of which remain genuinely contested — skeptical theism is a real philosophical position, not a dodge, but it has real costs (moral-skepticism spillover, unfalsifiability) that its critics have not been answered away. CSR explains *how* god-belief is cognitively easy to form without settling whether any god-belief is *true* (genetic fallacy risk is real and often ignored on the atheist side of pop debate).
- **[inference, my overall read]:** the empirical/scientific literature as a whole is much stronger at explaining *why humans believe* than at adjudicating *whether the belief is correct* — that adjudication mostly reduces back to the philosophical arguments (problem of evil, hiddenness, cosmological/design arguments not covered in this note) rather than to any single dataset.

# Evidence for God, and the impersonal alternatives

2026-09-13. Research synthesis. Detail sections in `notes/2026-09-13-god-evidence/` (5 files, every factual claim carries a URL; agent inferences flagged inline).

## The short version

There is no evidence for God in the sense you'd accept for any other empirical claim — no replicated measurement, no prediction that came out. What exists is a set of *philosophical* arguments about why there is anything at all, plus historical arguments about one first-century Palestinian execution. Both are genuinely contested by serious people. Neither moves a well-calibrated prior very far.

Three distinct questions get collapsed into one, and they need separating:

1. **Does something ground-of-being-ish exist?** Live question. Unresolved. Arguments on both sides are real philosophy.
2. **Is it a person who intervenes?** Much weaker. The empirical record is null-to-negative.
3. **Is it specifically the Christian God who raised Jesus?** A separate and much larger evidential leap that apologetics routinely smuggles in for free after winning (1).

The gap between (1) and (3) is the single biggest thing popular apologetics elides.

## What the professionals think

| Group | Theism | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Professional philosophers (PhilPapers 2020) | 18.93% | vs. 66.95% atheism |
| Philosophers of religion (2020) | ~78% | vs. 17% among non-specialists |
| AAAS scientists (Pew 2009) | 33% belief in God | 51% any deity/higher power |
| NAS members (Larson & Witham 1998) | 7% | elite selection effect |

The philosopher-of-religion inversion is the largest specialist effect in the entire PhilPapers survey. Draper & Nichols (*The Monist*, 2013) argue it is a selection artifact — people who already believe go into the field — which is exactly what you'd expect and which neuters that 78% as independent evidence. Note the symmetric worry: analytic philosophy may select for naturalism at the top of the funnel too. Neither number is diagnostic the way expert consensus is in, say, climate science.

## The arguments, ranked by how much they actually survive contact

**Fine-tuning** — the strongest. The constants really are narrowly poised; this is not apologist invention. But the reply space is live physics and live probability theory (multiverse + anthropic selection, the Ikeda–Jefferys result, White's firing-squad rebuttal, the normalizability problem — you cannot put a uniform prior over an unbounded parameter space). Verdict: unresolved, and unresolved in a way that involves real technical work rather than rhetoric.

**Contingency / PSR (Leibniz)** — the deepest. "Why is there something rather than nothing" is not a stupid question and "brute fact" is not obviously an answer. But the Principle of Sufficient Reason has to survive van Inwagen's modal-fatalism reductio, and it's not clear it does.

**Kalam (Craig)** — popular, weaker than its popularity. Hinges entirely on A-theory vs. B-theory of time. On B-theory the universe doesn't "begin to exist" in the sense the argument needs. That's a metaphysics bet, not a physics result.

**Moral argument** — weak link is clear: non-theistic moral realism (Wielenberg, Shafer-Landau) is a mainstream defended position that simply denies the key premise.

**Ontological (Anselm, Plantinga's S5)** — logically valid, dialectically inert. The possibility premise is exactly as contestable as the conclusion. Plantinga conceded as much.

**Religious experience (Alston)** — weakest as third-person evidence. The religious-diversity objection has no good answer even from sympathetic epistemologists: the same experiential machinery produces Krishna, Allah, and the Virgin Mary with equal subjective force.

**Psychophysical harmony** (Cutter & Crummett 2025) is the newest entrant and largely untested.

## The empirical record

This is where it gets one-sided, and you should weight it accordingly since it's the only part with actual data.

- **Intercessory prayer**: STEP (Benson 2006, n=1,802) found no benefit; the arm *told* they were being prayed for did worse. Meta-analytic g ≈ 0.10, non-significant. This is the cleanest test anyone has run of an interventionist God and it came back null.
- **NDEs**: AWARE/AWARE-II veridical-perception hit rate ~1 in 28–30. Naturalistic mechanisms fit better — Borjigin's dying-brain gamma surge, REM intrusion (60% in NDE-reporters vs. 24% controls).
- **Lourdes**: 72 recognized cures out of ~7,000 claims across 160+ years, against spontaneous-remission base rates. That is not an anomaly, it's the base rate.
- **Psilocybin** (Griffiths, n=36): reliably produces experiences subjectively indistinguishable from mystical encounter with the divine. A drug does it. That's a real problem for experience-based arguments.

Against: the *logical* problem of evil is conceded dead (Mackie 1982 — Plantinga's free will defence won). The *evidential* version (Rowe, Draper) and divine hiddenness (Schellenberg) are very much alive. Skeptical theism is the standard reply and it's expensive: it buys immunity from the evidence at the cost of moral skepticism generally.

Cognitive science of religion (HADD, Boyer, Barrett) explains why humans generate gods, which is not by itself a disproof — that move is a genetic fallacy without an added unreliability argument. But it does remove the "everyone believes it" datum from the pro column.

## On Christianity specifically

- **Jesus existed and was crucified**: near-unanimous among critical scholars. Mythicism is fringe. Paul's seven undisputed letters (50–60 CE) are the strongest early attestation; Tacitus and Josephus are genuine but partly interpolated.
- **Resurrection**: no consensus exists and by the historical method's own rules none can. Habermas/Licona's "minimal facts" vs. hallucination/legend accounts is a dispute about *priors*, not about data. Whoever sets the prior on miracles wins their own argument. This is the crux, and it's unresolvable by evidence.
- **Manuscripts**: transmission fidelity is genuinely excellent. It proves nothing about the truth of the contents. Apologetics blurs this constantly — it's the most common sleight of hand in the genre.
- **Archaeology**: confirms places and people (Pilate stone, Caiaphas ossuary, Pool of Siloam). Does not confirm Exodus, the conquest of Canaan, or the Quirinius census (which is chronologically broken).
- **Prophecy**: Daniel is near-universally dated after the events it "predicts." Matthew's virgin birth rests on the Greek LXX mistranslating Hebrew *almah* (young woman) as *parthenos* (virgin). Isaiah 53 is genuinely contested, not settled either way.

## The thing you actually asked about

"A universal subconscious we're all connected to." That intuition has a rigorous home, and it isn't Jung.

**Jung is not what you think.** The collective unconscious in Jung's mature writing is shared psychic *architecture* — inherited structural predispositions (archetypes) that make all humans generate similar symbols — not a shared telepathic mind. It's closer to "we run the same firmware" than "we're on the same network." Evolutionary psychology partly vindicates the structural claim and largely replaces the Lamarckian framing. Useful as phenomenology, unfalsifiable as metaphysics.

**The rigorous version is Bernardo Kastrup's analytic idealism.** One universal consciousness; individual minds are *dissociated alters* of it — the dissociative-identity-disorder analogy is load-bearing, not decorative. That is precisely your intuition, worked out with actual argumentative machinery. It's contested and speculative, but it's philosophy, not woo, and Kastrup has the technical background to defend it. Start here.

**Adjacent respectable territory**: panpsychism and cosmopsychism (Goff, Strawson) — motivated by the hard problem of consciousness, blocked by the unsolved combination/decombination problem. Spinoza's *Deus sive Natura* (Einstein's God — "I believe in Spinoza's God, who reveals himself in the lawful harmony of the world"), Plotinus' the One, Eckhart's Godhead, Tillich's Ground of Being, Whitehead's process theology. Eastern: Brahman/Atman (*tat tvam asi*), Tao, Buddha-nature — with Buddhist *anatta* as the sharp contrast case that denies any universal self at all.

**Pseudoscience, be clear about it**: Sheldrake's morphic resonance and Akashic records. No mechanism, no accepted evidence. The Global Consciousness Project and Radin's ganzfeld/presentiment work are unresolved parapsychology that hasn't survived rigorous replication — Bem (2011) and Wagenmakers' demolition of it is the canonical cautionary tale about how "significant" results get manufactured.

**What's actually established**: the unitive mystical experience is robust, cross-cultural, and now pharmacologically reproducible on demand. That is a real finding. It establishes that the experience is real. It does not establish the metaphysics the experience seems to reveal — and the psilocybin result cuts both ways here, since it shows the brain alone suffices.

Defensibility ranking for someone with your priors: Spinozan naturalism > academic panpsychism > Kastrup's idealism (most rigorous fit to your actual intuition) >> Jung-as-metaphysics > GCP/parapsychology >> morphic resonance / Akashic records.

## My read

If you want the honest bottom line: the case for bare theism is a philosophical stalemate that the empirical record tilts against, and the case for *Christianity specifically* is substantially weaker than the case for bare theism — it inherits every problem of the first plus a historical claim that turns entirely on your prior for miracles.

The "universal subconscious" framing is the more interesting thread for you, because unlike Christianity it doesn't require any historical claim to be true, and unlike interventionist theism it doesn't make predictions that have already failed. It's metaphysics rather than evidence — but it's metaphysics with live, serious defenders and an actual open problem (consciousness) motivating it, rather than an institutional inheritance looking for post-hoc support. Kastrup's *The Idea of the World* is the place to start if you want the argued version rather than the vibe.

One caution: "connected to everything" is doing two jobs in that intuition — a metaphysical claim (one substance) and a psychological one (the felt dissolution of self-boundaries). The second is well-evidenced and genuinely available. The first doesn't follow from it.

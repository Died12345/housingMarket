*2026-09-13. Research note. Question: what are the non-theistic/impersonal "God-equivalents" in psychology, philosophy, and religion, and which have any evidential standing — specifically assessing "a universal subconscious we're all connected to."*

## 1. Jung's collective unconscious

**What Jung actually claimed.** The collective unconscious is not a shared memory bank or telepathic mind-meld. Jung's mature position: archetypes are *inherited psychic structures/predispositions* — formal patterns for organizing experience (Mother, Shadow, Anima/Animus, Wise Old Man, Trickster) — not inherited *content* or memories. Archetypes are more like a river-bed that shapes how psychic "water" flows, common across humanity because the human brain/psyche has a common evolved structure, not because minds are literally linked. [Collective unconscious overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_unconscious), [IAAP on Archetypes of the Collective Unconscious](https://iaap.org/resources/academic-resources/collected-works-abstracts/volume-9-1-archetypes-collective-unconscious/).

- **The Self as God-image**: Jung: the Self "is a God-image, or at least cannot be distinguished from one" — but he's explicit the Self-archetype is *not* God, only structurally indistinguishable from how humans represent God/wholeness. The quaternity (fourfold mandala symbolism) is his recurring evidence for the Self as an organizing archetype of psychic wholeness. [IAAP: The Self](https://iaap.org/jung-analytical-psychology/short-articles-on-analytical-psychology/the-self-2/), [on quaternity/mandalas](https://carljungdepthpsychologysite.blog/2020/02/22/mandalas-5/).
- **Synchronicity**: "acausal connecting principle" — meaningful coincidence between an inner psychic state and an outer event, connected by *meaning*, not causation. The canonical example is the scarab-beetle case (patient dreams of a scarab, a rose-chafer beetle taps on Jung's window mid-session). This is Jung's attempt to gesture at something beyond strict causal science without claiming a physical mechanism. [Synchronicity overview](https://medium.com/@sparkcc94/synchronicity-by-carl-jung-b44aa597be39), primary text: Jung, *Synchronicity: An Acausal Connecting Principle* (Collected Works vol. 8), [JSTOR record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7s94k).
- **"I don't believe, I know"**: 1959 BBC *Face to Face* interview, John Freeman asked "do you now believe in God?" Jung: "I don't need to believe, I know." Context matters — Jung meant he had direct psychological experience of the God-archetype/numinous, which for him counted as a kind of empirical (psychic) knowledge, not a metaphysical claim that a deity literally exists outside the psyche. [Reporting on the quote](https://unveiltheworld.substack.com/p/carl-jung-i-know-that-god-exists).

**Critiques (this is where it gets weak, evidentially):**
- **Unfalsifiability**: if archetypes live in an inaccessible "psychoid" realm, no experiment can disconfirm them — a standard science-vs-pseudoscience red flag. [Critique summary](https://www.samwoolfe.com/2024/08/jungian-archetypes-collective-unconscious-unscientific.html).
- **The Lamarckian problem**: taken literally, "inherited collective unconscious" implies acquired experience passed down generationally — a mechanism genetics rejects. Scholarship is split on whether Jung himself endorsed this or was closer to a Baldwin-effect (selection for developmental plasticity, not inheritance of content) reading; the debate is real and unresolved in Jung studies. [Rensma 2013, *J. Analytical Psychology*, "did Jung believe in the inheritance of acquired characteristics?"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-5922.12008) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23550576/)); [Hogenson on the Baldwin effect in Jung](https://www.jungiananalysts.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Hogensen-The-Baldwin-effect_-A-neglected-influence-on-C.-G.pdf).
- **Replication status**: archetype theory has essentially no controlled, replicated empirical program comparable to modern psychology — it's a clinical-interpretive framework, not a tested one. Critics note cross-cultural mythic-motif "evidence" is also explicable by cultural diffusion/convergent narrative constraints, no collective-psyche needed. [Critical analysis](https://www.samwoolfe.com/2024/08/jungian-archetypes-collective-unconscious-unscientific.html).
- **Evolutionary psychology — partial rehabilitation, partial replacement**: EP reframes archetype-like patterns as evolved cognitive/behavioral modules (fear of snakes, kin-recognition, mate-preference heuristics, threat-detection) with actual selection-pressure mechanisms and testable predictions — this is a *naturalistic* substitute that keeps Jung's phenomenon (universal psychic patterns) while discarding his mechanism (mystical inheritance). Contemporary Jungians (Anthony Stevens, Christian Roesler) have tried to fuse the two frameworks explicitly. [ScienceDirect: "Algorithms and archetypes: Evolutionary psychology and Jung's collective unconscious"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/1061736194900132), [IAAP report on reconceptualizing archetype theory](https://iaap.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Report-Archetype-Theory-Roesler-1-3.pdf).

**Bottom line on the user's phrase**: "universal subconscious we're all connected to" is *not* what Jung's mature collective unconscious means. Jung's version is a shared *architecture*, not a shared *substance* or live telepathic network — closer to "we all have the same species-typical operating system" than "we're all plugged into one server." The version the user is describing (a literal shared field of mind) is much closer to Kastrup's idealism (§4) or to occult claims like the Akashic records (§5) than to Jung.

## 2. Eastern non-dual conceptions

- **Advaita Vedanta — Brahman/Atman, *tat tvam asi*.** Radical non-dualism: Atman (individual self) is numerically identical to Brahman (ultimate reality) — "you are that." Source: *Chandogya Upanishad* ch. 6 (~600 BCE), systematized by Shankara (8th–9th c. CE). This is arguably the closest premodern doctrine to "universal consciousness we're all part of," except it's explicitly *not* subconscious — it's the ground of consciousness itself, and full realization (moksha) is described as liberating, not merely explanatory. [SEP: Śaṅkara](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/), [IEP: Advaita Vedanta](https://iep.utm.edu/advaita-vedanta/), [Britannica: tat tvam asi](https://www.britannica.com/topic/tat-tvam-asi).
- **Tao (Taoism).** The Dao is explicitly *not* a mind or consciousness — it resists definition, glossed only as "Way"/"course"/"method." Primary text: *Daodejing* (Tao Te Ching), attributed to Laozi. Metaphysically it's closer to an impersonal generative principle/process than to a mind you're "connected to." [SEP: Laozi](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/laozi/), [SEP: Taoism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2006/entries/taoism/).
- **Buddha-nature / Dharmakāya.** In Mahayana tathāgatagarbha doctrine, all sentient beings possess innate potential for enlightenment (Buddha-nature); the Dharmakāya is the "truth body" — ultimate nature of reality itself, one of the Buddha's three bodies. This is the Buddhist concept structurally closest to a shared ground of mind. [Encyclopedia of Buddhism: Buddha-nature](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/Buddha-nature), [Tsadra Foundation: Dharmakāya](https://buddhanature.tsadra.org/index.php/Key_Terms/dharmak%C4%81ya).
- **Anatta (no-self) — the contrast case.** Theravada/early Buddhist doctrine denies any permanent self *or* universal Self — no atman, no Brahman-identity, just impermanent, conditioned psychophysical processes. This directly *rejects* the "universal mind we're all part of" framing; it's the position that there is no persisting subject at any level, individual or cosmic. Important because it shows Eastern thought is not monolithically pro-universal-consciousness. [SEP: Mind in Indian Buddhist Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mind-indian-buddhism/), [Encyclopedia of Buddhism: Anātman](https://encyclopediaofbuddhism.org/wiki/An%C4%81tman).

## 3. Western philosophical equivalents

- **Spinoza — *Deus sive Natura*** ("God, or Nature"). *Ethics* (1677): one infinite substance, God and Nature are the same thing under different descriptions; no personal, willing deity, no supernatural intervention — reality's own necessary structure *is* what "God" refers to. Einstein, replying to Rabbi Herbert Goldstein's 1929 telegram: "I believe in Spinoza's God, who reveals himself in the orderly harmony of what exists, not in a God who concerns himself with the fates and actions of human beings" — i.e., Einstein used Spinoza's God as a stand-in for lawful cosmic order, explicitly rejecting a personal deity. [Mind Matters summary with citations](https://mindmatters.ai/2021/12/einstein-believed-in-spinozas-god-who-is-that-god/).
- **Plotinus — the One.** Neoplatonism's ultimate principle: absolutely simple, beyond being and intellect, source from which everything else derives by atemporal "emanation" (not a temporal creation event). Intellect (Nous) is the first derivation, containing the Platonic Forms. [SEP: Plotinus](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2024/entries/plotinus/).
- **Meister Eckhart — the Godhead (*Gottheit*).** Distinguishes God (with attributes, will, personhood) from the Godhead behind God — an impersonal, ineffable ground "stripped of all goodness and being" that precedes even the God of theology. Eckhart's famous line: he prays to God to "make him free of God," i.e. to reach the Godhead beyond conceptual God. [SEP: Meister Eckhart](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/meister-eckhart/).
- **Paul Tillich — Ground of Being.** God is not *a* being among beings but "Being-Itself," the ground/power of being that everything participates in; explicitly a move away from theistic personalism. Key text: *The Courage to Be* (1952); *Systematic Theology* vol. 1. [Overview](https://www.philosophyprofessor.com/philosophers/paul-tillich/), [Religious Naturalism summary](https://religiousnaturalism.org/god-as-ground-of-being-paul-tillich/).
- **Process theology (Whitehead/Hartshorne).** God as fully temporal, changing, affected by and affecting the world in every moment ("dipolar" — a fixed "primordial nature" and a changing "consequent nature" that literally accumulates the world's experience). This is a live, still-debated academic research program (not just historical). [SEP: Process Theism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/process-theism/).
- **Hegel — Geist (Spirit).** Not an individual psychological state but a self-developing, self-conscious totality realized through history, culture, and mutual recognition between subjects — reality as Spirit coming to know itself through human minds collectively. Closest Western analogue to "a universal mind expressing itself through us," though Hegel's Geist is more a dialectical historical process than a static field. [SEP: Hegel](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel/).

## 4. Contemporary consciousness-based metaphysics

**Motivation**: the hard problem of consciousness (Chalmers, 1995) — no known reductive/functional explanation bridges physical processes and subjective experience ("why is there something it is like to be a brain doing X"). Panpsychism and idealism treat this as evidence that consciousness, not matter, is metaphysically basic. [SEP-adjacent: Chalmers "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness"](https://consc.net/papers/facing.pdf), [IEP: Hard Problem](https://iep.utm.edu/hard-problem-of-conciousness/).

- **Panpsychism** (Goff, Strawson): consciousness (or proto-consciousness) is a fundamental, ubiquitous feature of matter, present in some form even at the micro-physical level. Main objection: the **combination problem** — how do trillions of micro-experiences (electrons, quarks) combine into one unified macro-experience (you)? No accepted mechanism exists; Goff's "conceivability argument" specifically targets whether such combination is even intelligible. [SEP: Panpsychism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/panpsychism/), [SEP supplement: subject-summing/combination problem](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/panpsychism/supplement.html).
- **Cosmopsychism** (Goff 2017/2020, Nagasawa & Wager): inverts the direction — one single cosmic consciousness is fundamental, and individual minds derive from *it* rather than building up from particles. Faces the mirror-image **decombination problem** (Albahari 2020): how does one unified cosmic subject "un-combine" into distinct, apparently separate individual subjects like you and me? Unsolved; disputed whether it's actually easier than the combination problem or just relabels it. [PhilArchive: decombination problem](https://philarchive.org/rec/MILTDP-5), [SEP: Panpsychism, cosmopsychism section](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/panpsychism/).
- **Bernardo Kastrup — analytic idealism.** This is the closest rigorous academic analogue to what the user described. Kastrup's claim: there is one universal field of phenomenal consciousness ("mind-at-large"); individual minds (you, me, animals) are *dissociated alters* of it, analogous to alters in Dissociative Identity Disorder. Your brain doesn't generate consciousness — it's the *extrinsic appearance* (from outside) of a localized, dissociated process within universal mind; the physical world is what mentation looks like from a third-person vantage. Kastrup has a PhD in computer engineering and publishes in peer-reviewed philosophy venues, which distinguishes this from pop-spirituality, but it remains a minority, contested position within philosophy of mind — critiqued for relying heavily on an analogy (DID) whose disanalogies (DID alters share one brain/body; cosmic "alters" allegedly don't) are doing a lot of unexamined work. [Kastrup, "Analytic Idealism: A consciousness-only ontology" (PhilArchive)](https://philarchive.org/rec/KASAIA-3), [critical piece: "Why Kastrup's Analytic Idealism Fails"](https://ouroboricidealism.medium.com/why-kastrups-analytic-idealism-fails-e8e45e1671e2).
- **Integrated Information Theory (IIT, Tononi/Koch)**: a neuroscience-originated theory that consciousness = integrated information (Φ), which entails a panpsychist conclusion — even simple systems (a photodiode) have nonzero Φ, hence some minimal experience. This gives panpsychism its most "empirical-sounding" contemporary ally, but IIT itself is heavily contested: Scott Aaronson and others show it implies absurdly high consciousness for simple non-brain-like systems (grid-like error-correcting circuits), and a 2023 open letter signed by 100+ researchers labeled IIT's stronger claims "pseudoscience" for being empirically unfalsifiable at the relevant scale — this is a live, unresolved controversy in the field, not settled science. [PLOS Comp Bio: "The Problem with Phi"](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1004286), [IEP: IIT](https://iep.utm.edu/integrated-information-theory-of-consciousness/), [Searle/Koch-Tononi exchange summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/finding-purpose/202310/an-intriguing-and-controversial-theory-of-consciousness-iit).

## 5. Empirically testable versions — status check (blunt)

This is the section that separates science from belief. None of these support "we are all telepathically/subconsciously connected" as established fact; at best some show small, contested statistical anomalies that have not survived rigorous replication scrutiny.

- **Sheldrake's morphic resonance**: hypothesis that forms/behaviors are shaped by resonance with prior similar forms across space and time via non-physical "morphic fields," which would predict things like faster learning of a task once *anyone* has learned it. **Status: rejected by mainstream science.** No defined physical mechanism, studies criticized for lacking proper controls, and *Nature*'s editor called Sheldrake's founding book "the best candidate for burning there has been for many years." This is squarely pseudoscience by consensus, not a live controversy. [Wikipedia/critical summary with sourcing](https://www.samwoolfe.com/2013/07/the-problem-with-rupert-sheldrake.html), [PubMed: "Sheldrake's heretical hypothesis turns 40"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33751607/).
- **Global Consciousness Project (GCP)**: network of ~65 hardware random-number generators worldwide, hypothesis that RNG output deviates from randomness during moments of shared global attention (9/11, New Year's, etc.). Claims a cumulative ~7-sigma deviation over 17 years of testing ~500 events. **Status: methodologically contested, not accepted.** Critics note (a) no defined mechanism for how "collective attention" would physically affect a quantum/electronic RNG, (b) post-hoc event selection and multiple-comparisons issues (choosing which "global events" count, and the analysis window, after the fact), (c) independent reanalysis of the 9/11 data found results consistent with chance. This is the textbook shape of a p-hacking-vulnerable research program, not confirmed anomalous physics. [ScienceDirect: 17-year GCP retrospective](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1550830716302324), [Wikipedia summary of critiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Consciousness_Project).
- **Dean Radin — ganzfeld & presentiment meta-analyses**: ganzfeld (sensory-deprivation "telepathy" trials) shows a small, consistently-reported above-chance hit rate (~30-32% vs 25% expected) across decades of meta-analysis; presentiment studies claim physiological anticipation of random future stimuli. **Status: unresolved but trending negative.** Recognized methodological critiques: file-drawer/publication bias, inconsistent controls across the underlying studies, and Radin in particular criticized for "meta-meta-analyses" that aggregate heterogeneous, often low-quality study pools. A pre-registered Stage-2 Registered Report (2024, 40+ years of ganzfeld data) is the most rigorous recent attempt to settle this — check its actual conclusion before citing the effect as real. [PMC: Stage 2 Registered Report on Ganzfeld](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11134153/), [Skeptic's Dictionary on Radin](https://www.skepdic.com/essays/radin.html).
- **Bem 2011 "Feeling the Future" and the Wagenmakers rebuttal**: Bem's 9 experiments claimed evidence for precognition (retroactive influence on cognition/affect) using standard frequentist stats, published in a mainstream APA journal (JPSP). Wagenmakers, Wetzels, Borsboom & van der Maas (2011) reanalyzed with Bayesian methods and concluded the data provide no real evidence for psi — arguing Bem's frequentist approach was too permissive. Independent replication attempts largely failed. **This episode is widely credited as a major trigger for psychology's replication crisis and the push toward pre-registration** — it's now a canonical case study in *why* a single positive result in a peer-reviewed journal ≠ established phenomenon. [Wagenmakers et al., "The Case of Psi: Comment on Bem (2011)" (PDF)](https://web.stanford.edu/class/psych201s/psych201s/papers/Wagenmakers-etal-2011-bemComment.pdf), [background on the debate](https://atticusli.com/replication-crisis/daryl-bem-precognition/).
- **Akashic records**: claimed cosmic record of all events/thoughts, accessible via psychic "reading." **Status: no evidence base at all — not even contested science, just an unfalsifiable claim.** Term coined in the 1880s-90s by Helena Blavatsky/Theosophical Society, derived loosely from Sanskrit *akasha* (space/ether, one of the five classical elements in Hindu/Buddhist cosmology) but the "records" concept itself is a 19th-century occultist invention, not a classical Vedic or Buddhist doctrine. There is no proposed mechanism, no controlled study, no falsifiable prediction. [Wikipedia, sourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akashic_records), [Theosophy Wiki on origin](https://www.theosophy.wiki/en/Akashic_Records).

**Blunt summary of §5**: Sheldrake and Akashic records are pseudoscience by any reasonable standard (no mechanism, rejected/absent evidence, some direct disconfirmation). GCP and Radin's ganzfeld/presentiment work occupy the "parapsychology" zone — small, persistent statistical effects that have never cleared the bar of independent, pre-registered replication free of researcher-degrees-of-freedom criticism; the field's own most rigorous recent attempts (Registered Reports) are the right thing to check before citing either as evidence. Bem/Wagenmakers is the clearest case where the scientific process actually worked — a strong claim, published in a top journal, got dismantled by methodological scrutiny within a few years.

## 6. What mystical experience research actually supports

This is the one area with genuinely solid, replicated empirical grounding — but note *exactly* what it establishes.

- **Stace's phenomenology + Hood's Mysticism Scale**: W.T. Stace (1960) proposed that a core "unitive" experience — sense of oneness with all things, loss of ego boundary, noetic quality (feels like direct knowledge), sacredness, ineffability — recurs across religious traditions independent of doctrine. Ralph Hood operationalized this as the 32-item Mysticism Scale (M-Scale), factor-confirmed (introvertive unity, extrovertive unity, interpretation) in the US and cross-culturally, including with Iranian Muslim samples. **This shows the *phenomenological structure* of unitive experience is a robust, measurable, cross-cultural psychological phenomenon.** [Confirmatory factor analysis, US/Iran](https://academia.edu/10086029/Dimensions_of_the_Mysticism_Scale_Confirming_the_Three-Factor_Structure_in_the_United_States_and_Iran), [comparative study across Christian/Muslim/Hindu students in India](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-5906.2010.01508.x).
- **Psilocybin-occasioned mystical experience (Griffiths et al., Johns Hopkins)**: 2006 double-blind study found high-dose psilocybin reliably occasions experiences meeting full Stace-type mystical-experience criteria in most participants; 2008 follow-up found these experiences retained high personal-meaning and spiritual-significance ratings at 14 months, with ~94% of participants reporting the session increased wellbeing/life satisfaction. **This is a real, replicated, peer-reviewed pharmacological result: a specific brain-state reliably produces the unitive experience on demand.** [Griffiths et al. 2006, *Psychopharmacology* (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16826400/), [Griffiths et al. 2008 follow-up](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0269881108094300).

**The key epistemological point (not an inference, a logical distinction)**: the *experience* of unity — feeling merged with all things, dissolution of self/other boundary — is a real, robust, cross-culturally consistent, and now pharmacologically reproducible psychological event. That a brain state reliably produces the *feeling* "I am one with the universe" tells you the feeling is real and has neural correlates. It tells you **nothing** about whether the underlying metaphysical claim (that you *actually are* metaphysically continuous with a universal mind/Brahman/God) is true. A drug or meditation practice reliably causing the *sensation* of contacting cosmic consciousness is exactly as consistent with "there is a universal consciousness and altered states let you perceive it" as with "the brain has a specific, evolutionarily-explicable circuit for generating ego-dissolution/oneness feelings, full stop, with no external referent." The data underdetermine the metaphysics. Anyone who tells you the psilocybin studies "prove" universal consciousness is overclaiming; anyone who dismisses the phenomenology as therefore meaningless is also overclaiming in the other direction.

## 7. Which of these are defensible, ranked

Flagging explicitly: this ranking is my own synthesis/judgment call, not a citable consensus — treat it as informed opinion, not fact.

1. **Spinoza's Deus sive Natura / naturalistic pantheism.** Most defensible for a technically-minded skeptic: makes zero claims beyond what physics already asserts (the universe has a lawful, necessary structure), just relabels that structure "God." Costs nothing, adds no untestable entities. Einstein's usage is the model.
2. **Panpsychism (Goff/Strawson) as a solution to the hard problem.** Serious, published, argued rigorously in mainstream analytic philosophy (SEP has a full entry). Motivated by a real explanatory gap (hard problem), not by wishful thinking. Main cost: the combination problem is genuinely unsolved — you're trading one hard problem for another, not solving anything outright.
3. **Jung's collective unconscious, read as archetypal/structural (not literal-telepathic).** Defensible as a *psychological/interpretive* framework with real clinical and cultural-analytic utility, weak as *science* (unfalsifiable core, Lamarckian baggage in places). Best treated as a metaphor partially cashed out by evolutionary psychology's evolved-cognitive-module research, which is the empirically tractable descendant.
4. **Mystical-experience phenomenology (Stace/Hood/Griffiths) as psychological fact, agnostic on metaphysics.** Fully defensible and evidence-backed *as a claim about human experience and neuropharmacology*. Becomes indefensible the moment you upgrade "people reliably feel unity" to "therefore a universal mind exists."
5. **Cosmopsychism / Kastrup's analytic idealism.** Intellectually serious (peer-reviewed philosophy, addresses the hard problem head-on), and the closest real position to "one universal (sub)consciousness we're all part of." But it's a minority position resting on a contested inference (idealism solves the hard problem better than physicalism) and an analogy (DID) doing more work than its disanalogies comfortably allow. Hold this if you want the user's stated intuition dressed in its most rigorous available form — but hold it as speculative metaphysics, not established fact.
6. **Advaita Vedanta / Brahman-Atman, Eckhart's Godhead, Tillich's Ground of Being, process theology.** Coherent theological/philosophical systems with long traditions of internal argument, but they're frameworks that presuppose or argue *for* a metaphysical thesis rather than derive it from evidence — properly in the domain of philosophy of religion, evaluated on conceptual coherence and argument quality, not empirical support.
7. **IIT-flavored "consciousness is fundamental physics."** Interesting but currently unresolved even within its own field — treat as an open scientific controversy (note the "pseudoscience" open letter), not a settled underpinning for panpsychism.
8. **GCP, Radin's ganzfeld/presentiment.** Genuinely tested empirically, and genuinely not holding up under the most rigorous scrutiny available (pre-registered Registered Reports, Bayesian reanalysis). Fair to call these "unresolved parapsychology" rather than confirmed phenomena — don't cite as evidence for connection between minds.
9. **Sheldrake's morphic resonance, Akashic records.** Not defensible on current evidence. No mechanism, no accepted replication, direct scientific pushback. These are the "just belief, dressed in science-adjacent language" end of the spectrum — call them what they are.

**Direct answer to the original framing**: there is no evidenced "universal subconscious" in the sense of a literal shared psychic network you can tap into. What *is* real: (a) all humans share evolved cognitive architecture producing similar symbolic/behavioral patterns (Jung's actual, defensible claim, better explained today by evolutionary psychology); (b) altered states reliably produce a *feeling* of universal connection, which is a genuine, measurable brain phenomenon; (c) a minority of serious philosophers (Kastrup, cosmopsychists) argue on independent grounds (the hard problem) that consciousness may be metaphysically fundamental and individual minds derivative of one universal mind — this is live, rigorous, unresolved philosophy, not confirmed fact, and it's the most honest home for the intuition being asked about.
